Screenr AI | Changelog – August 4 – August 18, 2026

✨ Features

• **Manager Review — the Hiring Pipeline Now Completes End to End**: Managers can record the final hiring decision in-product, closing the last gap between a scored interview and an actual outcome. Three conclusions are supported — advance to a final human interview, hire directly, or reject — each routed to its state through a pure rule layer, so adding a decision without routing it is a compile-time error rather than a runtime surprise. Every decision is logged as a recruiter action with a mandatory written rationale (20-character minimum, since the transition log is the only place the reasoning survives for a future reviewer). The panel deliberately shows no composite score of its own: each stage already renders its evidence independently, and a rollup number on the decision screen would quietly become the thing people decide on. Candidates who reach `hired` now receive a congratulations email, closing the loop on the one outcome that previously ended in silence. (`src/lib/rules/manager-review.ts`, `src/lib/actions/manager-review.ts`, `src/components/candidates/manager-review-panel.tsx`, `src/lib/services/email-templates/hired.ts`)

• **Proctoring Detection Brought In-House — Zero Per-Interview Cost**: Camera proctoring no longer calls a hosted vision API. A committed YOLOX-tiny model (Apache-2.0 licensed, ~20MB) scores frames in memory on our own infrastructure and discards them, so per-frame cost goes to zero and no candidate imagery crosses a third-party boundary. Ultralytics YOLO11 was evaluated and rejected on licensing grounds — its AGPL-3.0 terms would have attached a source-disclosure obligation to Screenr. The same detection pass now also identifies a visible phone, not just how many people are in shot. (`agents/interview/src/detector.ts`, `agents/interview/src/postprocess.ts`, `agents/interview/models/`)

• **Interview Recording Retired in Favour of a Tighter Privacy Posture**: The AI interview is no longer recorded. The recording service, storage bucket, playback UI and associated credentials were removed; the candidate's camera now exists only as a live track that is sampled in memory for proctoring and discarded. The durable record is the transcript, the score and the proctoring report — a deliberate privacy position, documented so it isn't reversed by accident. (`supabase/migrations/20260804140000_drop_interview_recordings.sql`, `src/lib/services/livekit-egress.ts` removed)

• **Camera Proctoring for the AI Interview, Tab-Focus Proctoring for Voice Screening**: The interview worker samples the candidate's video every ~10 seconds and reports what it observes to a secret-guarded endpoint — server-side evidence a candidate's machine cannot forge. Frames are kept deliberately out of the interviewer's own session, so the interviewer never reacts to what the camera sees mid-call; monitoring stays separate from the conversation. Voice screening gained tab-focus monitoring, closing the one candidate-facing stage that had no coverage at all — it is also the stage with the most incentive to look elsewhere, since the question is asked before the answer is given. Both stages run the same rules and render through one shared report component, so findings read identically wherever they came from. (`agents/interview/src/vision.ts`, `src/app/api/agent/interview/proctoring/route.ts`, `src/components/candidates/proctoring-report.tsx`)

• **Evidence Snapshots — Every Camera Finding Is Checkable**: Because interviews are no longer recorded, a flagged frame is preserved as a single small annotated image so a recruiter can verify what the system reported rather than taking it on trust. Storage is tightly bounded by design: only flagged frames are captured (a clean interview stores nothing), at most one image per condition per 30 seconds, and at submit everything outside a confirmed finding is deleted — so the images behind the detector's own false positives are precisely the ones discarded. Images live in a private, owner-scoped bucket and are served through short-lived signed links. (`agents/interview/src/snapshot.ts`, `src/app/api/agent/interview/snapshot/route.ts`, `supabase/migrations/20260804160000_proctoring_snapshots.sql`)

• **Structured Rejection Reasons — Outcomes Are Now Countable**: Every closing decision now carries a structured code alongside its written rationale, replacing free text that varied between automatic and human paths. Questions like "how many candidates did we reject on resume score this quarter?" are answerable directly from the database rather than by reading rows by hand. Enforced centrally — the single function every state change passes through refuses a closing transition without one, so no future code path can skip it — and backed by a database constraint and an index built for aggregate reporting. Managers choose their own code when rejecting, since "the evidence was weak" and "the evidence was fine and I decided otherwise" are materially different facts worth being able to count separately. (`src/lib/data/transitions.ts`, `src/lib/constants.ts`, `supabase/migrations/20260807120000_transition_disposition_codes.sql`)

• **Live Detection Overlay on the Candidate's Self-View**: Candidates can see the detection boxes drawn on their own camera preview during an interview — a transparency choice, so monitoring is visible rather than hidden. It cannot influence the record: the boxes travel one way only, and the stored report is assembled server-side from the worker's own readings. The overlay refreshes faster than the report samples, kept as two separate settings so display smoothness can never change what a stored report means. (`src/lib/proctoring/overlay.ts`, `src/components/realtime/video-interview.tsx`)

🛠️ Improvements

• **Interview Length Tuned to 10 Minutes**: The AI interview was shortened from 20 to 10 minutes and is now paced by a question budget rather than a clock instruction — a real-time model has no reliable sense of elapsed time, so budgeting questions is what actually holds the duration. The prompt version was incremented so scores remain interpretable against the conversation that produced them. (`src/lib/services/interview.ts`, `src/lib/constants.ts`)

• **Detection Tuned to Under-Report Rather Than Over-Report**: Proctoring is deliberately calibrated so that a genuine candidate is not wrongly flagged — the costlier error by a wide margin. Low-quality frames are discarded rather than trusted, a finding requires several consecutive sightings before it counts, and detections too small to be a real person in the room are rejected outright. Frame quality is measured directly (lighting and contrast) rather than taken from the model's own self-report, so a poorly lit room reads as "couldn't see clearly" instead of "candidate absent." (`src/lib/proctoring/incidents.ts`, `agents/interview/src/postprocess.ts`)

• **Interview Audio Quality Protected Under Load**: Vision inference was moved onto its own worker thread, off the loop that carries the live interview audio. Worst-case interruption to the audio path dropped from 58ms to 13.1ms, well inside the tolerance for a natural-sounding conversation. The detection thread also recovers automatically from a crash, so a single bad frame degrades to a brief gap in coverage instead of ending camera monitoring for the remainder of the interview. (`agents/interview/src/detector-worker.ts`, `agents/interview/src/vision.ts`)

🔒 Security / Correctness

• **Candidate Interview Links Hardened**: The candidate side of the interview flow now performs its privileged reads and writes only after the signed link token has been verified, using a dedicated server-side client — matching the boundary the platform's security model always specified. Candidates have no accounts by design, so this is the mechanism that lets a valid link work while an invalid one reveals and changes nothing. (`src/lib/actions/interview.ts`, `src/lib/data/candidates.ts`)

• **Monitoring Systems Report Observations, Never Verdicts**: All severity judgement is centralised in one pure, versioned function, so every candidate is assessed by identical rules and the reasoning is auditable. The reporting endpoints accept no severity and no finding type — a component holding the shared secret can submit raw observations but cannot manufacture an accusation. The candidate's browser is likewise unable to either fabricate or suppress a camera finding, and a malformed submission from a browser no longer discards the server-side evidence alongside it. (`src/lib/proctoring/incidents.ts`, `src/lib/proctoring/collector.ts`)

🐛 Bug Fixes

• **Interview links reported as inactive for valid tokens**: A query read a candidate field from the wrong table, which caused the whole lookup to fail and surfaced to candidates as "this link is no longer active" even on valid, unexpired invitations. The same pattern elsewhere would have silently prevented interview auto-scoring. Both corrected and covered by tests. (`src/lib/data/candidates.ts`)

• **Interview sessions occasionally started without an interviewer**: The interview and screening agent workers were registered in a way that let the platform route a room to whichever worker it happened to select — measured at 3 of 6 interview rooms being picked up by the wrong worker. Both workers now register under distinct names and are dispatched explicitly, making room assignment deterministic. (`agents/interview/src/agent.ts`, `agents/screening/src/agent.ts`, `src/lib/services/livekit.ts`)

• **Per-question voice scores and storage cleanup**: Storage bucket removal now runs through the Storage API rather than a database migration, where that state does not live; and the interview agent package declares its own type dependencies rather than relying on the root workspace. (`supabase/migrations/20260804140000_drop_interview_recordings.sql`, `agents/interview/package.json`)

📝 Notes

• **Deployment requirements for this release.** Both agent workers must be restarted so they register under their new names — a worker left on the previous build stays in the automatic dispatch pool and will continue picking up the other flow's sessions. The disposition-code migration must be applied (`supabase db push`) before this deploys, as the database functions are called with parameters the currently deployed versions do not accept. The new reporting constraint is intentionally applied to new records only: existing transition rows predate the codes and are an immutable audit trail, so they are left as-is rather than back-filled with reasons nobody recorded.

• **In progress, not in this release.** The same candidate-link hardening applied to the interview flow above is being extended to the voice screening flow, where the identical pattern was found during review. The fix is written, tested and open for review; it is scheduled for manual QA in a clean browser session before merge.

• **Flagged, not built this period.** Cleanup of proctoring images currently runs on the interview-submit path, so a session abandoned mid-way can leave images in place until a follow-up adds cleanup to every terminal state — tracked as a follow-up issue. The camera sampling loop has been validated in full against its test suite; end-to-end validation against a live webcam (specifically: a person crossing the frame, and a candidate leaning out of shot briefly — neither of which should raise a finding) is worth completing before enabling camera proctoring for real candidates. Two smaller refinements are noted for later: one detection setting reads counter-intuitively (lowering it makes the report more conservative, not less), and annotations are currently baked into stored images, so they cannot be restyled retrospectively.
