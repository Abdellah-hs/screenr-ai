Screenr AI | Changelog – August 18 – August 31, 2026

*79 commits · 445 files changed · 15 database migrations · test suite grew from 114 to 176 files*

---

✨ Features

• **Every AI Score Is Now Reproducible — No Model Ever Returns a Number**: All three scored stages — résumé, voice screening, AI interview — stopped asking the model for a 0–100 figure. The model now reports a plain evidence label per criterion (`not present` / `unclear` / `weak` / `partial` / `strong` / `very strong`) together with the exact words it read, and Screenr derives every number from a fixed table in code. The reason is simple: "is this a 68 or a 74?" has no stable answer, so the same résumé could score differently on two consecutive runs. A reading repeats; an arbitration does not. The ladder is shared across the three stages, so a "strong" reading is worth the same wherever it was read — a recruiter comparing a résumé score to a screening score is finally comparing two numbers made the same way. What each level *means* is deliberately kept per stage: a CV proves a skill by listing a role and a duration, an interview answer proves it by what the candidate can say about the work when pressed. (`src/lib/scoring/evidence-levels.ts`, `src/lib/resume-scoring/`, `src/lib/screening-scoring/`, `src/lib/interview-scoring/`)

• **Résumé Requirements Are Gates, Not Weights**: The recruiter's only per-criterion choice is now **Must have** or **Nice to have**. Every must-have is checked independently and all of them must pass; every failure is reported, not just the first. A ranking score is calculated only for a candidate who cleared every requirement — an ineligible candidate gets no number at all, rather than a low one that would read as "how close they came" and invite an argument with the gate. This closes a real hole: the old weighted total let a surplus on one criterion quietly pay for a shortfall on a non-negotiable one, turning "must" into "mostly". A nice-to-have can now never repair a failed must-have, by any route — that invariant is what the whole module exists to hold, and the tests assert it directly. Quoted evidence is verified against the exact document the model was shown; an unverifiable quote earns no credit. (`src/lib/resume-scoring/`, `supabase/migrations/20260819160000_resume_evidence_scoring.sql`)

• **The Screening Rubric Finally Decides the Screening Score**: Until this release a recruiter could build, weight and maintain a screening rubric that *nothing read* — the score was an unweighted average per question. Questions are now drafted **from** the rubric, and the rubric dimension is the unit that gets graded, read across the whole transcript. This matters because a candidate who demonstrates a competency while answering some other question has still demonstrated it, and because the old approach silently gave a competency probed by two questions twice the influence of one probed by a single question — a weighting nobody chose, produced by phrasing. The rubric also decides how many questions get asked (one per dimension, between 3 and 8), so no dimension is left unprobed and no question is spent on something nothing grades. (`src/lib/screening-scoring/`, `src/lib/services/screening-questions.ts`, `supabase/migrations/20260822120000_screening_dimension_scores.sql`)

• **The Interview Rubric Was Never Read At All — Now It Is**: The interview prompt told the model to pick its own competencies, and the caller fetched only the rubric's *version number*, to stamp the score with. So a rubric a recruiter had built and weighted decided nothing, while the stored score implied it had. The interview now grades against the recruiter's actual rubric. It also does one thing differently on purpose: a competency the interview never reached is **left out** of the score rather than scored zero. Interview questions are improvised from the candidate's own CV — which is exactly what makes the evidence hard to bluff — so nothing aims a question at each dimension, and scoring an unreached one zero blamed the candidate for a question nobody put to them. What that gives up is disclosed rather than hidden: how many dimensions were actually covered is calculated, stored and shown above the breakdown, because "100 from one competency of five" and "80 from all five" must not look alike. (`src/lib/interview-scoring/`, `src/lib/services/interview-scoring.ts`)

• **Teammates Assigned to a Campaign Can Now Actually Open It**: Campaign reviewers, the editor for them, and the role list all shipped in the very first schema migration — and for five months nothing referenced them. Assigning a colleague let them read the row saying they were a reviewer, and nothing else, while the interface implied full access. There is now one access ladder, enforced in the database rather than only in the interface: **observer** reads everything on a campaign, **reviewer** additionally decides (move a candidate, re-score, write audit entries), **lead** additionally configures (rubrics, questions, timers, availability, the reviewer list), and **owner** additionally deletes the campaign. Forty-seven policies across sixteen tables were rewritten, and the central state-change function was updated with them — without that, the rest would have been cosmetic, because it performed its own owner check and would have refused a reviewer no matter how the policies read. (`supabase/migrations/20260830140000_campaign_reviewer_access.sql`, `src/lib/rules/campaign-access.ts`)

• **The AI Audit Trail Is Readable and Exportable**: Every AI call has been logged with its raw output, model, prompt version, rubric version and rationale since the pipeline shipped — and nothing ever read it back. With recruitment AI classified as high-risk under the EU AI Act, an audit trail nobody can inspect does not satisfy the requirement it was built for. There is now an audit screen filterable by campaign, stage and date range, with any row expandable to the raw model output and the exact prompt and rubric versions behind it, plus CSV and JSON export. Exports are re-queried on the server rather than assembled from what the browser sends back — an export is evidence, and evidence built from client-supplied content is not evidence. Recruiter decisions are paired onto the row alongside the AI evidence that preceded them. (`/admin/audit`, `src/lib/data/audit.ts`)

• **A Candidate File Now Shows Its Own History**: The transition log has been complete, append-only and trustworthy since the state machine shipped, and no screen ever displayed it. Every state change a candidate has been through is now rendered as a timeline, with automated steps and human decisions drawn as visibly different marks — the previous single dot made a rule firing and a recruiter deciding look identical, which quietly erases the claim the whole product rests on. Where a person's decision reversed the direction the pipeline was heading, that is labelled as an override. (`src/lib/rules/transition-timeline.ts`, `src/components/candidates/activity-timeline.tsx`)

• **Scores Link Straight to the Words Behind Them**: Both spoken stages were verifying the model's quoted evidence and then throwing the quote away, so a manager saw a number with nothing behind it. The quote is now stored and rendered inline under each dimension, with a link that jumps to that moment in the transcript in context — so a decision can be checked without replaying a call. Grounding a score and locating a quote are treated as two separate questions on purpose: only grounding may reduce a score, so adding this display feature could not quietly start demoting scores that used to stand. (`src/lib/scoring/evidence.ts`, `src/lib/scoring/quotes.ts`)

• **Curated Talent Pool, Separate From the Directory**: `/candidates` used to return everyone who ever applied — a useful directory, but not the opt-in "worth revisiting" list the product promised. Both now exist under honest labels. A recruiter marks someone deliberately, from the candidate page at any stage or from an unticked checkbox on the reject dialog (pre-ticking it would refill the pool with everyone ever rejected, i.e. the directory again). Entries carry tags and free-text notes, and are searchable by name, skills, tags, original campaign, date added and best score reached at any stage. The pool deliberately outlives the campaign that filled it — closing a role must not quietly empty the pool it fed. (`supabase/migrations/20260819140000_talent_pool_entries.sql`, `src/lib/talent-pool/search.ts`)

• **Bulk Actions on the Candidate Table**: Candidates can be selected and moved or rejected together. A bulk advance is still N individual validated state changes, never one blanket update — so the system tells you *before* you press the button that "advance 40" will really advance 12, and why each of the others was skipped. Two things are deliberately excluded: bulk moves take people *up to* a decision point, never through one (one rationale spread across fifty candidates is not fifty judgements), and a state whose only next step is a system-sent email is excluded so a bulk action cannot fabricate a message nobody received. (`src/lib/rules/bulk-actions.ts`)

• **Non-Responsive Candidates Leave the Pipeline On Their Own**: Campaigns can set a window after which a candidate who never responded is archived automatically, with a way back. Archiving was previously reachable only by a recruiter changing the stage by hand, with no window and no recovery. The default is off for every existing campaign — a sweep that silently began archiving people the moment it deployed would be the wrong kind of surprise for a state that removes people from view. One window covers all the non-responsive endings, because from the recruiter's side they are the same fact. (`supabase/migrations/20260819120000_auto_archive_window.sql`, `/api/cron/auto-archive`)

• **Overdue Work Is Visible Where the Work Happens**: SLA timers, breach rules and counts all existed — but only inside a notification bell, with no way to act on them. Lateness is now a badge and a filter on the candidate table, a banner on the campaign page, and the top group of the overview's queue, all computed from a single shared definition so the bell and the list can never disagree about the number. Notification links land the recruiter on exactly the filtered set they clicked. Finished candidates are never flagged: someone stopped moving because a decision was made, which is the opposite of the stuck condition an SLA exists to catch. (`src/lib/rules/sla.ts`)

• **Candidate Interview Prep Guide**: A web page candidates receive with their interview invitation, telling them what the format is and what to expect. It is mobile-first even though the interview itself is desktop-only — it is opened from an email people read on their phone, and it is where they learn they will need a laptop, so it has to work on the device that tells them they need a different one. It describes the *experience* of being challenged, never the interviewer's tactics: repeating those would be a manual for defeating the probe, and a test asserts the agent's own phrasings never reach candidate-facing copy. (`/prep/[token]`)

• **Final-Interview Reminders Now Actually Send**: The 24-hour and 1-hour reminder email has existed since spring with nothing driving it. A scheduled sweep now sends them. A pure rule answers "what is owed right now", so the job stays correct at any frequency; at most one email goes out per pass, and when a gap leaves both unsent the nearer one is sent and the stale one retired rather than delivered late. Reminders are claimed before sending so two overlapping runs cannot double-send, rescheduling resets both, and a candidate rejected the day before their interview is never reminded to attend it. (`src/lib/scheduling/reminder-sweep.ts`, `/api/cron/interview-reminders`)

• **Campaign Creation Is a Five-Step Wizard**: Role → Rules → Rubric → Team & timing → Review, with no sidebar, a step rail that checks off what is done, and a Next button that says what is still owed instead of going dead. Screening questions moved into creation — they were the only setup step that happened afterwards, so every campaign was born in the exact state the detail page has to warn about: no questions means no candidate can be approved into screening, while the apply link is already live and collecting CVs. The final step previews what the campaign will actually do to a candidate, counting how many steps run without you and how many wait for a person. Two genuine bugs surfaced and were fixed along the way: a rubric with one unnamed dimension was silently discarded entirely on save, and a timer whose escalation landed after its own limit could never escalate. (`src/app/(focus)/campaigns/new/`, `src/lib/campaigns/step-blockers.ts`)

• **Full Interface Redesign — Colour Now Means Consequence**: Sixteen commits rebuilt the recruiter and candidate surfaces on one rule: **the AI advises in indigo, a person decides in ink.** Four colour families, one job each — near-black for "a person is about to change someone's state", indigo strictly for machine output (a rail or a caption, never a button), blue for navigation and focus and never for an action, emerald only for a positive final outcome. Concretely: the campaigns list answers "which one do I open next" with a pipeline bar and a single worst-thing-waiting column; the overview leads with a queue of people rather than four numbers going up; a candidate file pins the decision and the three stage scores beside the evidence instead of nine hundred lines below it; the candidate table survives 200 rows with a sticky header, denser rows and aligned figures; and every dropdown shares one row definition so the next menu cannot be smaller than the last. Missing values are never a blank dash — "Screening expired", "No show", "Awaiting the call" and "Awaiting your approval" are four different facts with four different next actions. (`src/app/globals.css`, `src/components/ui/`)

• **The Voice Screening Call Is Now Driven By the App, Not the Model**: The screening worker was rebuilt as a single state machine — seven states, one pure decision function, one queue — replacing a dozen loose flags each written by whichever callback happened to notice the thing it described. The app decides every question and the interviewer only speaks it; the model cannot start a turn on its own. This closes a failure that was invisible from outside: an interviewer choosing its own questions holds a perfectly normal-sounding conversation that evidences no rubric dimension, so the candidate scores zero across the board and nothing in the record says why. The interviewer also no longer talks over the candidate, the candidate sees their own answer countdown, and they can press "I'm done" to move on early. (`agents/screening/src/machine.ts`, `/api/agent/screening/control`)

• **Candidates Choose the Call Language Before It Starts**: Picked in the browser rather than negotiated mid-call, from a closed list on both sides. Choosing wrong is fixable by simply starting the call again. The interviewer is forbidden from raising the subject at all — asking, offering to switch, or confirming mid-call is what produced the language-flipping this replaces. (`src/lib/screening/instructions.ts`)

• **Proctoring Findings Now Come With the Picture**: A flagged frame was being photographed and then deleted seconds later, so a recruiter reading "phone visible" had nothing behind it — the exact failure the feature exists to prevent. Two causes, both fixed: the camera was sampled too infrequently to measure a short event accurately, so an ordinary glance was rounded down below the reporting bar and pruned along with its image; and each image was filed under only one finding, so a frame showing both a phone and a second person attached to one and left the other bare. Images now attach to every finding they depict, at no extra storage. (`agents/interview/src/vision.ts`, `src/lib/proctoring/incidents.ts`)

• **Re-Score an Interview Without Sitting It Again**: Interview scoring previously had exactly one trigger — the candidate's own submission — so an interview graded under the old rules was stuck there forever. A re-score button refreshes the evidence and deliberately applies no state change, because the application has usually already moved on and re-running the decision would either fail or shove a candidate a manager is actively reviewing back into the queue. It refuses only a closed application: rewriting the evidence behind a decision already made only muddies the record. (`src/lib/actions/interview-scoring.ts`)

• **Contact Links Are Read Off the CV, Not Asked For**: A CV whose contact block is three icons was returning no links at all, because a URL hidden behind the word "LinkedIn" is not prose and the extractor is told to invent nothing. A deterministic reader now harvests them. It can only ever fill a blank, never overwrite something the model or the candidate supplied, and every URL it returns appears verbatim in the document. GitHub gained a proper column alongside LinkedIn and portfolio — a candidate is a person, and two of the three links they carry had a home while the third did not. (`src/lib/resume-ingest/contact-links.ts`, `supabase/migrations/20260823160000_candidates_github_url.sql`)

• **An Outage of Ours Costs the Applicant a Delay, Never Their Application**: If the résumé extractor timed out or a third party returned an error, nothing was written at all — nobody at the company ever knew somebody had applied, and the applicant received an email asking them to upload a clearer file. Their file was usually fine. Failed intake now files the applicant properly, using the name and email they typed on the form themselves, in an explicit failure state that is visible and contactable and can never be scored. They receive the ordinary "we've got it" receipt, because they *are* received. A one-click repair re-reads the stored CV and returns them to the normal queue — and it is deliberately the only failure state with a way back, because it is the only one that records something we did rather than something the candidate did. (`src/lib/resume-ingest/reprocess.ts`, `src/lib/rules/processing-failure.ts`)

• **Screening Questions Are Checked Against the Rubric Before a Campaign Goes Live**: A rubric competency no question asks about scores zero for every single candidate, and that zero enters the total as though they had failed it — when nobody asked. A check names the unprobed competency before launch and blocks, with both remedies stated in the message: ask about it, or take it out of the rubric. If the check itself cannot run, the campaign still advances with a visible note — a service being down says nothing about the questions, and a configuration warning must never become an outage. (`src/lib/services/screening-coverage.ts`)

🛠️ Improvements

• **One Threshold Became Two, Because They Were Never the Same Number**: A single box labelled "Screening Threshold" was setting the auto-reject line on two different stages. A recruiter raising the bar to stop weak CVs was silently also raising the bar on candidates who had already answered well — and a résumé ranking and a spoken-answer grade are not comparable quantities. They are now two separate settings, backfilled so no existing campaign's line moved on deploy, and the résumé decision structurally cannot reach for the screening bar. The default was also settled: the database said 50 while the form sent 70, so a campaign created through the form and one created any other way rejected at different bars. (`supabase/migrations/20260821120000_split_resume_screening_thresholds.sql`)

• **The Screening Threshold Advances People — It No Longer Rejects Them**: Falling below the screening bar now rests the candidate for a person to look at, instead of automatically rejecting them. Three reasons: the volume saved was tiny (the résumé stage already cuts the pile before a screening link is ever sent, so this saved a recruiter a handful of review items at the price of never letting a person look at someone who held a live conversation with the product); it contradicted the rule one stage later, where a full interview never auto-rejects; and the screening score is the most fragile number in the product, so a missed rubric gap became a silent stack of rejections rather than a queue somebody notices. The waiting state was added to the notification bell in the same change — replacing an auto-reject with a queue nobody can see would be worse than the problem it fixed. (`src/lib/rules/screening-response.ts`)

• **"Must Have" Is a Résumé Control, and Nowhere Else**: The rubric editor offered Must have / Nice to have on the interview stage, with a caption saying those dimensions knock a candidate out. Nothing did — the interview never rejects at any score, so an interview must-have could not knock anyone out even in principle. The control named a rule that does not exist and the caption asserted it outright. It now appears only on the résumé stage, which is the only place it is both enforced and checkable against a document. The generator is forced to clear the flag on the other stages in code, not merely asked to in the prompt — no editor control renders it there, so a recruiter could never see a stray one, let alone clear it. (`src/components/campaigns/rubric-editor.tsx`)

• **"Required" Was Removed From Screening Questions**: The flag gated nothing — no rule read it and no submission was blocked by it. Worse, it was working against the scoring: the voice interviewer was told optional topics were "if time allows", so it could legitimately skip one, while the overall score counts every topic — meaning a skipped question scored zero and dragged the candidate down. The flag was quietly telling the interviewer that costing someone points was acceptable pacing. The interviewer is now told to cover every topic, and told why. (`supabase/migrations/20260821140000_drop_screening_question_is_required.sql`)

• **The Campaign Persona Now Reaches the Interviewer**: Four interview personas were selectable, validated and stored — and the value never left the database, so every interview ran identically no matter what was chosen. That is worse than an unbuilt feature: a recruiter who picked "Pressure" got a neutral interview, and the stored setting was a false record of how the candidate was actually interviewed. The default persona emits a byte-identical instruction to before, so interviews recorded before this stay comparable. (`src/lib/interview/instructions.ts`)

• **The Middle Tier Reads "Potential Match"**: A copy change only, no data migration. "Moderate" reads as a verdict on the candidate, where the tier is only a band derived from a score. The rename surfaced a surface that built its own label out of the raw database value and would have kept saying the old word — a test now asserts no component derives a tier label for itself, so a screen cannot silently opt out of the next rename. (`src/lib/constants.ts`)

• **The Typed-Answer Screening Path Was Retired**: A second, unexercised route into the same scoring pipeline, re-enabled by one environment variable. Typed answers are exactly the copy-paste-gameable input that voice screening exists to remove, and a scored record looked identical either way with no signal saying whether the answer was typed or spoken. Removed in full — the form, the submit action, its validation, its data writer and its rule. (`src/app/respond/[token]/`)

• **The Team-Reviewers Editor Is Behind a Flag, Default Off**: It creates placeholder identities for people who have no Screenr account, so it writes reviewer rows for users who cannot log in. The rows are harmless — the new access checks match on a real signed-in identity, which a placeholder never is — but a reviewer list full of people who can never see the campaign is worse than no list. It stays hidden until reviewer invitations create real accounts. The server refuses the work too: hiding a form section only removes its inputs. (`src/lib/flags.ts`)

• **Candidates Are Told What Happens to Them, Before It Happens**: Both live stages opened with logistics and left the three things a candidate is actually anxious about unstated — whether they are recorded, whether a bad first take is final, and whether a machine decides. All three have good answers in this product and were only discoverable by finishing the call. The expired-link page was the worst of it: "this link has expired, please contact the hiring team" reads as a door closing, and a candidate who missed a deadline assumes they have been rejected. They have not been, and the page now says so. (`src/app/respond/[token]/`)

• **Interview Slots Show in the Candidate's Own Timezone**: Every slot was rendered in the interviewer's zone with the zone name in a chip beside it, asking a candidate in Copenhagen booking a New York interviewer to convert in their head — where the cost of getting it wrong is missing the interview. Times now show in the reader's own zone with a toggle, and the confirmation quotes both clocks, since the time is the one thing a candidate will re-read later and the zone is exactly what they will forget. (`src/app/schedule/[token]/`)

• **Documentation Brought Back In Line With the Code**: Seven long-planned features were formally retired with the reason recorded per section, rather than left as commitments nobody was working on. The main project guide was restructured from 3,216 lines to 1,696 — it was documenting nine mechanisms that had been deleted and citing a file that does not exist — with the historical reasoning moved intact into a dedicated decision record that opens by saying what no longer exists. One retirement is explicitly recorded as retiring a *feature*, not an *obligation*: the EU AI Act places duties on the deployer, and nothing currently monitors for adverse impact, so removing the section must not be mistaken for having decided the question. (`CLAUDE.md`, `docs/prd.md`, `docs/decisions/voice-screening-worker.md`)

🔒 Security / Correctness

• **Anonymous Candidates Could Not Complete a Screening Link**: Candidates have no Screenr account by design, but the entire screening flow ran through the signed-in database client. Every table it touched is scoped to the campaign owner, so an anonymous visitor matched no policy: the queries succeeded and returned nothing, and a perfectly valid link surfaced to the candidate as "we couldn't find this application." The flow now verifies the signed link first and then does its privileged work through a dedicated server-side client, matching the boundary the interview flow already drew. Tests pin the contract so a future helper cannot quietly go back to the session client. (`src/lib/actions/respond.ts`)

• **Interviewer Instructions Were Readable by the Candidate**: Both workers read their instructions from room metadata, and three code comments claimed the candidate could not touch them. Only half of that was true — the server alone can *set* metadata, but the platform *delivers* it to every participant. A candidate with the browser network tab open could read the confidential screening topic guide before being asked anything, and at the interview stage the condensed copy of their own CV and the campaign's chosen persona, so they knew how hard they were about to be pressed. Metadata now carries only the application identifier — the one thing the worker cannot learn any other way and the one thing that costs nothing to expose, since the candidate's own link already encodes it. Each worker fetches its instructions from a secret-guarded endpoint it was already authenticated against, so the trust boundary gained no new trust. Nothing is stored: the instructions are rebuilt on demand, so there is no second copy to drift. (`src/lib/screening/instructions.ts`, `src/lib/interview/instructions.ts`)

• **A Dead-End Stage Was Removed From the State Machine**: A reference-check stage was a legal destination in the state machine with nothing built behind it — no screen, no action, and no rule to move an application onward. A candidate sent there by the stage dropdown would sit until a person noticed by hand, which is the silent failure the architecture forbids everywhere else. Removed end to end, with any existing row remapped to manager review. A recruiter phoning a referee is unaffected and needs no stage: that happens while the application sits in manager review, which is already the point where things wait for a person. (`supabase/migrations/20260830120000_drop_reference_check_state.sql`)

• **Access Checks Do Not Confuse "Yours" With "Current"**: Found while reviewing the access migration before applying it. The new role lookup filtered out soft-deleted campaigns, which the check it replaced never did — making it a behaviour change dressed as a permissions fix, and a data-losing one. The talent directory deliberately reads a campaign's removal status so it can *flag* a removed campaign while still listing the person; with the filter in place, the directory would have quietly lost everyone whose only campaign had been removed. Access control answers "is this yours", not "is this current"; hiding removed campaigns stays with the queries that should not show them. (`src/lib/data/campaigns.ts`)

• **Monitoring Endpoints Still Accept Observations Only**: The proctoring reporting routes accept no severity and no finding type from any caller, browser or worker. All judgement remains in one pure, versioned function, so every candidate is assessed by identical rules. Live detection boxes shown on the candidate's own preview travel one way only and cannot reach the stored report. (`src/lib/proctoring/incidents.ts`)

🐛 Bug Fixes

• **Candidates who never showed up for an AI interview sat in the pipeline forever.** The expiry state existed in every layer and no code anywhere wrote it: the deadline lives *inside* the signed link, so verification threw on a dead link and nothing downstream ever ran — the only code that knew a link was dead also refused to say whose it was. A link can now be authenticated without enforcing its deadline (the signature check is unchanged, so a forged link still fails), which is what lets both the candidate-triggered path and a nightly sweep close the invitation out properly. A live interview is never swept mid-call. (`src/lib/auth/screening-token.ts`, `src/lib/interview/expiry-sweep.ts`)

• **The expiry sweeps were fully built, tested — and never scheduled.** Only the calendar-watch renewal was listed in the deployment schedule, so neither the screening nor the interview sweep ever ran in production. Expiry was therefore detected only when a candidate reopened a dead link, which is exactly the population that never comes back. Five jobs are now scheduled and staggered so they never overlap. (`vercel.json`)

• **Scored interviews stopped dead in fully-automatic campaigns.** Every earlier stage advanced on a rule and this one silently did not, so a campaign explicitly set to run automatically parked finished interviews indefinitely. They now advance to manager review — deliberately without a score gate, because rejecting someone who sat a whole interview on the strength of one number is the decision most worth keeping human. (`src/lib/rules/interview-scoring.ts`)

• **Cloned campaigns had no working apply link.** The clone path never minted a public slug, so a cloned campaign's apply page was unreachable. Backfilled for existing rows and fixed at the source. (`supabase/migrations/20260823120000_backfill_cloned_campaign_slugs.sql`)

• **The contact-link backfill filed employer websites as candidates' portfolios.** Its first version took "the first URL in the document that isn't on a denylist", which on a real CV is almost never the candidate's own site — it is a previous employer's homepage or a university's. Those values were reverted, and a bare domain now only counts for LinkedIn and GitHub, where the shape is unmistakable; anything claiming to be a portfolio must spell itself out in full. (`supabase/migrations/20260823170000_undo_backfilled_portfolio_links.sql`)

• **Two score columns had never been written to since the schema was created**, while the real stage scores lived with the evidence that produced them. Dropped, so nobody reads a permanently empty column and concludes a stage was not scored. (`supabase/migrations/20260823140000_drop_unused_stage_score_columns.sql`)

• **A finished screening call could be abandoned without submitting.** When the interviewer finishes and says goodbye, the answers are now submitted automatically and the candidate lands on the done screen. Previously an interview somebody wandered away from sat unsubmitted until the expiry sweep closed it out — so a candidate was rejected for a call they had actually completed. The trade-off is recorded and accepted: a candidate with a poor microphone has no second attempt after the interviewer signs off, and the transcript is kept either way so a recruiter can see what happened and re-send. (`agents/screening/src/machine.ts`)

• **Row menus were clipped inside the candidate table**, because the stage-change menu was positioned against its nearest scrolling ancestor. It now renders above the table entirely. The last column also stopped being a chevron that linked where the row already linked, and became a real per-row menu built on the existing move flow — so the written reason and the log entry come along unchanged. (`src/components/ui/anchored-menu.tsx`)

📝 Notes

• **Deployment order matters this release.** Both agent workers must be deployed **before** the app. Each worker fetches its instructions from the new endpoint and falls back to the old metadata path, so a new worker runs against either version — but an old worker against the new app finds no instructions and strands the candidate in a silent room. Fifteen migrations must be applied before deploy, including the access-role rewrite, which changes how forty-seven policies evaluate.

• **Old scores are not comparable to new ones, and are deliberately not back-filled.** A résumé ranking, a screening score or an interview score written before this release was produced by different arithmetic against a different unit. Re-score to move a candidate onto the current rules; the stored version stamp on each score is what says which rules produced a given figure. A stored score should show the unit it was actually graded in, not the unit today's code would use.

• **An objection was raised and overruled on proctoring sensitivity.** The bar for reporting a visible phone or a second person was lowered from fifteen seconds to five, at the product owner's direction, so an ordinary glance is reported. This weakens the deliberate bias toward missing incidents rather than inventing them, which matters more here than almost anywhere because interviews are not recorded and a finding cannot be checked against footage — a person genuinely walking past behind a candidate now registers. What keeps it proportionate: a single stray frame still spans zero time and can accuse nobody, two sightings is a warning rather than a serious finding, the serious bar is unchanged at thirty seconds, and every finding now arrives with the frame attached so a recruiter judges a picture rather than an assertion. Revisit this if reports start reading as noisy. A diagnostic mode was added to settle the related question of why a held phone is sometimes not detected at all — three quite different causes look identical from outside, and lowering a threshold for the wrong one adds false accusations without catching anything.
